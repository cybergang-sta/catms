ABSTRACT
The purpose of this project was to design and implement a mobile-based classroom allocation system to address
persistent scheduling challenges within the Information Systems and Technology Department of the University
of Technology and Applied Sciences (UTAS). The objectives were to investigate the current manual classroom
allocation system and identify its challenges, to develop a computerized solution capable of automating
classroom allocation with timetable management, and to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed solution in
reducing scheduling conflicts.
The study adopted the Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM), complemented by an Agile software
development technique, to iteratively design, develop, and test the system. Requirements were gathered
through questionnaires administered to students. The software was implemented using Hypertext Markup
Language (HTML), Cascading Style Sheets (CSS), JavaScript, PHP, and MySQL, and evaluated through unit
testing, integration testing, and user acceptance testing. Findings revealed that the system eliminated double
bookings, automated class allocations efficiently, and improved communication timetable by providing real-
time notifications. Evaluations with departmental staff and students indicated high usability (over 85% of
participants found the system easy to use), scheduling accuracy exceeding 90%, system response times under
2 seconds, and 99.5% uptime during the evaluation period.
The significance of this project lies in its ability to enhance administrative efficiency, reduce human error,
and provide equitable access to classroom resources. Academically, it contributes to research on mobile-first
solutions in higher education, while practically, it offers a scalable model for adoption across other departments
and technical universities in Ghana. It is recommended that the institution consider extending the system to
other faculties to further improve resource management across the university.

Introduction
1.1
Background of the Study
The scheduling and allocation of lecture rooms and considering class timetables is an important task in higher
educational institutions. As numbers continue to rise globally, the challenge for academic departments to
effectively use their teaching resources is compounded. Manual scheduling, using spreadsheets or paper-based
systems, are often not effective because they require significant human resource, risk human error and do not
accommodate last-minute changes. Some recent research has shown that digital scheduling applications and
mobile-enabled promotional applications have significantly improved educational management by offering au-
tomation, efficiency, and real-time responsiveness (García-Peñalvo et al., 2023; Li & Zhao, 2024; Musiimenta
et al., 2025). Not only do scheduling technologies change practice on the use and allocation of classrooms, but
they are giving students and university lecturers access to continually updated timetables on their individual
mobile devices in Beene’s with global directions towards mobile-first educational management.
In many African universities, often including Ghana’s University of Technology and Applied Science (UTAS),
the persistence of manual class allocation systems creates ongoing issues. The Computer Science Department,
recently experiencing a sharp increase in the number of students enrolled, regularly face issues of timetable
overlaps, classroom double booking, and dissemination of updated timetables. These issues do not only disrupt
academic activities, causing missed lectures and uptake students to experience poorer levels of satisfaction and
staff productivity. Research from institutions in sub-Saharan African research indicates that the absence of
digital scheduling platforms impedes academic quality and impacts institutional competitiveness (Agyapong &
Teye, 2023; Osei & Boateng, 2024; Okafor et al., 2025). In contrast, institutions that utilize digital tools report
improvements in space use, decrease in administrative workload and improved stakeholder communication.
This research indicates a pressing need for UTAS to use a more reliable and a mobile based allocation with
local context and global best practices.
The purpose of this project is to design and implement a mobile-based class allocation system that automates

learning while also providing a framework that can be adapted in other departments or institutions with similar

digitizing of schedules, broadly improved accuracy and efficiency of scheduling, and improved accessibility
on sites, the Computer Science department at UTAS has never fully embraced any digital scheduling options
in any practical sense, thus hindering the potential to further automate the scheduling process.

Chapter Two
Literature Review
2.1
Introduction
This chapter reviews existing literature on classroom allocation systems, examining both traditional and
computerized approaches. It discusses the advantages and disadvantages of each approach and presents related
work in the field.
2.2
Overview of Classroom Allocation System
There are classroom allocation systems, also known as scheduling or timetable systems, that are designed to
assign courses, faculty, and student cohorts to appropriate classrooms and time slots, to facilitate scheduled
academic activities successfully and efficiently. Classrooms and classroom allocation practices have generally
been manual processes where administrators prepare timetables to use available resources. However, manual
practices involve human error such as course timetable clashes, double-booked classrooms, and wasted
facilities, and can hinder the teaching and learning process. It would be difficult to manually generate a detailed
timetable to accommodate the increasing enrolments of students and the variety of courses (Owusu et al., 2023;
Mensah & Boateng, 2024).
To develop these complex schedules, many institutions have implemented automated classroom allocation sys-
tems that optimize resource utilization and employ algorithms to schedule courses, student cohort assignments
and faculty to various classrooms across academic terms and semesters. Automated systems can account for
the number of students enrolled in a course, the instructional needs of the course, what a faculty member
needs to teach the course minus any scheduling conflicts, and the number of students that can occupy the
classroom. These tools do not only minimize the occurrence of academic timetable clashes, but they also ensure
more efficient management of academic administration in various educational institutions. Current advanced
automated classroom allocation systems incorporate mobile and cloud-based technology to provide schedules
6


===== PAGE 18 =====
to students and instructional staff members in real-time, improve communication between students and
faculty, and enhance the availability of scheduling software, all while streamlining academic administration.
These tools also provide improved flexibility and responsiveness to organizational demands within higher
education (Adeyemi et al., 2023; Kusi & Adjei, 2024). The advancements in classroom allocation to include
mobile technology is a significant advancement towards higher education management. Mobile technology
systems allow students and faculty to view live and historical class schedules and update these schedules on
their smartphone mobile applications that would reduce delays in communications in real-time. Adopting
mobile technology eases accessibility to students in the determined instructional/learning environment where
noticeboards and printed schedules are typically ineffective. Mobile technologies align with seeking to utilize
more advanced technology and in their intentions of digital transformation across global education systems
that would support the whole institution in adapting more effectively to new learning environments (Nkansah
& Arthur, 2024; Zhang et al., 2025).
2.3
Traditional Classroom Allocation System
The standard models of classroom allocation used in many educational systems still involve manual allocation
systems, whereby administrators organize classes to each room and times using paper files, spreadsheets, and/or
notice boards. For a long time, this has been a common method of allocating classes in many institutions,
particularly in developing countries, because it is not technically advanced or particularly expensive to
establish. Nonetheless, it is labor intensive in a sense that consumes a lot of time by a person, and many errors
may arise from it, causing class overlap when scheduling, double-booking rooms to appear on schedules, and
missing the opportunity to consider the number of students occupying available classrooms (Owusu, Danso, &
Lamptey, 2023; Adeyemi, Bello, & Johnson, 2023). The time-consuming nature of this process caused some
inefficiency that noticeably stole potential productivity to conduct learning, especially as the student population
increased.
Another delimitation of conventional classroom allocation model is how rigid they can be. This rigidity is
felt when a sudden change occurs - be it the lecturer is no longer available, the course has accepted additional
students, the students failed to bring their required technical items (like a laptop) and the rooms failed to be held
in high enough regard by the learners; the manual allocation model does not permit flexibility to quickly change
s system, and some of these uncontrollable changes confuse the learners and the lecturer, wasting precious
time and learning effectiveness (Mensah & Boateng, 2024; Nkansah & Arthur, 2024). Newer modern digital
7


===== PAGE 19 =====
systems allow for real time changes to be updated to learning systems, whereby the older conventional models
are generally restricted in terms of communication - whether by notice board or teacher-directed forms of
communication were used, the transfer of the information to the user to cause change is made more complicated,
decreasing overall efficiency.
Additionally, as the population requesting higher education has increased, and new varied programs of study
have been introduced, traditional convention classroom allocation systems have been challenged. Increasing
demand and growth in course offerings has driven the rise in sophistication of now bigger institutions than
before and human-operated manual systems have become inefficient in their capability to chart all of the
courses, and students, and rooms. This now compounding issue risks limited equity of the space and time
used by larger groups of classes, as underutilization and over-population of students in rooms occur. As cited
in recent studies, the inefficiency of these models can have an impact in not only poorly impacting the academic
delivery of a course, but also in increasing student disloyalty and stress (Education and Technology in Ghana,
2024; Zhang, Chen, & Li, 2025). In conclusion, while the conventional classroom allocation models once
benefited institutions of learning, the now changing size and pace of education suggest, amongst other things,
these systems require automated mobile-based educational platforms.
2.4
Advantages of Traditional Classroom Allocation System
(i)
Simplicity and Ease of Use: The traditional classroom allocation system is simple and does not depend
on individuals understanding the web and technical tools. Administrators can work timetables with pen,
paper, and noticeboard, and it is easy to understand and manage. This can sometimes be a benefit for
those institutions that do not have chronically technology-savvy staff members, and do not use have a
digital source to rely on.
(ii)
Reduced Costs for Implementation & Maintenance: As traditional allocation system is a paper
system, and does not require any software, servers, or any internet connection to operate, the cost of
maintaining the paper records is low. The only things you need to operate the system are office supplies
and noticeboards to display. For schools and departments with limited or available budgets, there are
few cheaper options to consider for planning the use of classroom space.
(iii)
Flexibility to make immediate changes: Manual systems enable administrators to manage time-
sensitive changes as they arise. For example, if a lecturer was unable to teach their class, or, if a room was

no longer able to be used, the administrator can change the timetable and modify arrangements without
waiting for a computerized decision. When compared to digital or automated systems, sometimes the
administrator’s ability to respond is quicker than making the necessary correction in a computerized
setting.
(iv)
Independence from Technology: Traditional allocation systems are immune to power interruptions,
system failures, or internet outages. Institutions that have had many technical interruptions (or
interruptions that interrupted others) to their traditional processes, could be academically productive
regardless of their lack of reliance on the digital world. This could lead to increased resiliency in
environments where technology is frequently not reliable.
(v)
Human Oversight and Discretion: An administrator develops a timetable based on their experience,
and can continue to use their discretion, or professional knowledge, in regard to their timetable decisions.
For example, an administrator can consider each individual lecturer’s preferences, students’ needs, or
special conditions that a technical system may not capture or could affect if programmed. The human
element creates a perceived impression of a system that may more respond to need.

Disadvantages of Classroom Allocation System
(i)
Time-Consuming assignment process: Timetabling goes through the process of putting the timetable

2.6
Computerized Classroom Allocation System
Computerized classroom allocation systems were developed to improve upon the inefficiencies of manual
scheduling. Computerized systems use algorithms and database management systems to assist in the allocation
of classes to rooms by considering the class size, lecturer availability, and room capacity. Computerized
systems reduce things like timetable clashes, double-bookings, and under-utilization of facilities (Agyapong &
Darko, 2023; Li, Wang, & Zhou, 2024). Computerized systems also are more structured than manual systems,
making it easy for the administrator to store, retrieve, and update schedules.
One of the biggest advantages of computerized allocation systems, besides the speed of process completion, is
that computerized systems can easily adapt to changes in the environment and provide real, or near-real-time
alerts. Events such as lecturer absenteeism and student enrollment often occur unexpectedly. Computerized
systems allow for adjustments that can be quickly communicated to and updated for stakeholders when
events happen in real time (Owusu, Asante, & Boakye, 2024; Zhang & Liu, 2024). Prompt coordinated
communication represents time savings for everyone and provides better coordination for lecturers and
students. Additionally, computerized systems often support integrated institutional portals or web-enabled

Computerized classroom allocation systems promote performance and institutional efficiency through data-

This data can allow informed policy decisions and better resource allocation through infrastructure planning for
universities (Aminu & Bello, 2023; Chen, Wu, & Yang, 2025). In summary, computerized classroom allocation
systems represent not only more efficient scheduling, but an eventual path for improved future academic
planning and resource efficiencies. By using computerized systems, decisions on how to improve scholarly
activities may more readily occur, and more advanced and mobile-based classroom allocation solutions can be
reached.

participation and this mode of accessing content is set to foster in how class timetable are accessed. The
increase in mobile apps in higher education highlights the potential of their design for fostering more student

3.1
Design Science Research Methodology
This study refers to Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM) as the overarching structure for examining
and addressing the challenges of class allocation within the University of Technology and Applied Sciences.
DSRM is especially pertinent as its rationale is to introduce and evaluate new artifacts that are created to solve
real-world problems. The artifact in this situation is a mobile-based class allocation system. The research
methodology prescribes a structured six-step process: problem identification, define objectives, design and
development, demonstrate, evaluate, and communicate. The purpose of the DSRM is not only to examine
the problems surrounding the inefficiencies of the prior allocation system developed by the University, but
to ensure that the recommended practical solution through the research process is scientifically rigorous, and
relevant for the stakeholders of the University.
Along with DSRM, during the implementation of the design, the study adopts an agile software development
lifecycle approach. Agile methodology emphasizes iterative development, ongoing collaboration with
stakeholders, and a willingness to adapt, making it well-suited for projects requiring rapid feedback and
improvement. Through short cycles of software development (sprints), class allocation system features were
designed, developed, tested, and modified according to feedback by students, teachers, and administrators. The
proposals from the iterations were thoughtful and relevant, which allowed the researcher to combine DSRM
and Agile by using a structure presented by DSRM, while allowing for iterative and flexible improvement for
their project.

3.4.1
Requirement Elicitation and Analysis
Requirements Discovery
The requirements elicitation and analysis process for the mobile-based classroom allocation system involved
determining the needs of the stakeholders, such as the administrators, lecturers, and students, so that the system
could provide an answer to the problems with the manual system. Various methods, such as interviews and
observations were used to capture both functional requirements (as in automated class allocation, timetable
management, and notifications) and non-functional requirements (such as usability, security, reliability, and
scalability). The requirements were analyzed, and prioritized, with very essential needs incorporated into the
system in the beginning, such as allocation for classes with conflicts and notifications, and other less urgent
requirements kept for future development. The elicitation tools (use case diagrams and process flow diagrams)
also allowed us to validate and rework the requirements and provided the stakeholders with a visual means
16


===== PAGE 28 =====
to evaluate the plans of the system. The process was carried out using an Agile approach, which allowed for
flexibility, involvement of the stakeholders, and consistency with the institution’s overall goals.
Requirements Classification and Organization
Once the information was obtained using questionnaires, the functional and non-functional requirements
of the mobile-based classroom allocation system were categorized and organized to facilitate the design
and development process. The requirements were categorized into functional requirements, which included
classroom allocation automation, timetable creation, real time notifications, user registration/authentication,
and administration management, as well as non-functional requirements, which included usability, reliability,
security, scalability, and operational efficiency. To facilitate effective implementation of the mobile-based
classroom allocation solution, the requirements were prioritized as requirements that were high, medium,
and low based on how important they were to accommodate the challenges with the existing paper based
manual system. The high priority elements were resolved first. These included the ability to schedule each
room simultaneously so that there were no conflicts (e.g., a class booking the same room) and provide real
time updates to information within the app. To map each requirement to modules of the system, visual tools
were utilised such as requirement matrices and tables as a visual way to display each requirement. These
visual tools made it easier to ensure requirements traceability and clarity. The classification, organization of
requirements and Agile software development approach ensured a user-centered and stakeholder needs solution
was completed in the end and the final product operated reliably under a variety of conditions.
Requirements Prioritization and Negotiations
The prioritization of the mobile-based classroom allocation system requirements utilized the MoSCoW
approach, which sorts requirements into distinct categories as follows: Must-have, Should-have, Could-have
and Won’t-have. A Must-have requirement would include features that were vital to the application, like
automated classroom allocation, conflict-free scheduling, and notifications, since these requirements address
the main issues being faced by students, lecturers, and administrators of the allocation system. A Should-have
requirement would be timetabling exports or basic reporting, since they are useful features but not critical for
the first version. A Could-have requirement would be personalization options and advanced analytics, which
are desirable improvements but can be developed later. Won’t-have requirements were identified during our
stakeholder engagement as features that lay outside the project or are not required for phase 1. The MoSCoW-

constraints which enabled the development team to focus on delivering maximum value in the first release of

5.1
Concluding Remarks
This chapter serves as the final summary of the project to design and develop a Classroom Allocation System
for the University of Technology and Applied Sciences. It concisely presents a conclusion of the project which
discusses the degree to which the objectives of the project were met. It also details the major contributions of
the project, the limitations of the project and the recommendations for future upgrades and deployment which
can be realistically achieved. The last chapter of this project aims to conclude and encapsulate the project while
representing its value and describing a way forward to continue planning the project development and how it
can eventually be contributed to the real world.
The project successfully investigated the current manual classroom allocation system, confirmed the existence
of significant challenges (timetable clashes, double-booking, delayed communication), and delivered a
functional mobile-friendly web-based solution. The system met its core objectives of automating allocation,
providing real-time notifications, improving resource utilization, and supporting administrative decision-
making.
Evaluation results confirmed high usability, accuracy above 90%, and strong stakeholder satisfaction.
5.2
Recommendations
The Classroom Allocation System has delivered satisfactorily. This report makes the following recommenda-
tions that could be considered by the University for the continued development of the features, security, and
long-term sustainability of the system:
(i)
Integration: It is strongly recommended that future work considers developing a secure API with
priority to enable the Classroom Allocation System to easily integrate with the university’s central
35


===== PAGE 47 =====
Student Information System (SIS) so that the importing processes for student lists, course codes and
lecturer assignment can be automated, avoiding the need to re-enter new and existing data points during
allocation, and keeping both services in sync.
(ii)
Notifications system: A notifications system (email/SMS) that could be easily integrated in the program
to provide scheduled updates to students and lecturers of newly allocated rooms, schedule changes and
pre-bookings.
(iii)
Enhanced features and User Interface: The interface could be enhanced to provide drag-and-drop
behavior for easy manual adjustments in the timetable. A stand-alone mobile application for this
classroom allocation system would be a significant step forward.
(iv)
Security: To make sure that the system is extremely secure, it is recommended to put other security
restrictions, like password policy and segment access to those sensitive data points with role-based hand-
off.
(v)
Scalability and performance: Finally, equally necessary to ensure the long-term sustainability of any
future system is awareness of the need for load and stress testing to help account for the full user base
of a department for the classroom allocation system, thereby keeping the system responsive to busy use
times for the demands of, like the beginning of a new semester.
